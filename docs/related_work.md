# Related Work & Novelty Assessment

Prior-art survey and novelty verdict for the project's claim: **RL gait
co-adaptation to a tunable parallel elastic element on a commercial rigid
humanoid (Unitree G1), scored in electrical (copper-loss / CoT) terms, with a
post-hoc-vs-in-loop methodology.**

> **Provenance.** Generated by the `deep-research` workflow (47 agents, 15
> primary sources fetched, 71 claims extracted, 24 confirmed / 1 refuted).
> **Methodology caveat:** verification was 1-vote (lightened from 3-vote after
> the run twice stalled on the API rate limit), so robustness is below the
> harness default — but every confirmed claim traces to a primary
> arXiv/IEEE/OSTI source with a verbatim quote. This is a fast-moving area

## 1 · Executive summary (the verdict)

The proposed study's individual ingredients are **all established prior art**,
but their **specific conjunction appears unclaimed as of June 2026**:

- *Parallel-elastic torque offloading cuts motor energy* — foundational
  (Plooij & Wisse 2012, clutched knee-extensor task). **[Figure DISPUTED:** an
  adversarial re-check (2026-06-14) traces Plooij & Wisse 2012 IROS to ~20%
  energy reduction on a 2-DOF arm, not the ~80%/66% quoted here — the 80% may
  belong to a different clutched-PEA Plooij work. Verify the primary before
  citing a number.**]**
- *Torque-squared electrical (copper-loss) accounting for a PEA, validated on
  real hardware* — done, but on a **quadruped** (Bjelonic/ETH RA-L 2023, ANYmal).
- *RL with elastic actuators for legged locomotion* — done, but on
  **quadrupeds** and methodologically distinct (Bjelonic/DLR 2022; PIL/Go2 2026).
- *A tunable parallel spring optimized with the torque-squared metric* — done by
  **this project's own group** (Belov/Osokin Skoltech 2024), but with closed-form
  analytic optimization + a fixed PD controller on a 3-DOF leg test-stand — no
  RL, no humanoid, no walking gait.

**Honest novelty verdict: integration/application novelty, not a new mechanism
or principle.** The unclaimed cell is the *combination* — commercial humanoid
platform + true in-loop RL co-adaptation + electrical accounting. Credible for
**RA-L / ICRA / IROS / Humanoids**. **T-RO / IJRR / Science Robotics would
require substantially more**: hardware validation, real G1 motor constants, and
ideally spring-parameter co-optimization (not a single hand-tuned spring).

Two papers will draw "incremental" reviews unless explicitly differentiated:
**Bjelonic 2023** (RL vs our in-loop retrain; BO-over-design-conditioned-policy
vs single fixed-spring retrain; quadruped vs humanoid) and the group's own
**Belov/Osokin 2024** (analytic vs RL; leg-stand vs walking humanoid).

## 2 · History of the field (mid-level)

**Spring-mass foundations.** Legged efficiency theory rests on elastic
energy storage (Alexander, Raibert spring-mass / SLIP), realized in hardware as
series (SEA) or parallel (PEA) compliance.

**SEA vs PEA is condition-dependent** (Kashiri 2018 review; Verstraten):
series compliance is often more efficient for shock/peak-power, but **parallel
compliance wins when supporting quasi-static / gravity torque** — and crucially
for a *copper-loss* study, **parallel springs change motor torque (cutting Joule
heating quadratically), whereas series springs in a backdrivable system barely
change motor torque**. This directly justifies the project's PEA choice. A
lockable parallel-elastic knee prosthesis (Geeroms 2017) measured 65 J/stride /
100 W peak vs 156 J/stride / 400 W direct-drive and 98 J/stride / 200 W SEA.

**Clutched / selectively-engaged parallel springs** are the canonical
energy-savers: Plooij & Wisse 2012 (clutched knee-extensor rebound rig; the
~80%/66% figure is DISPUTED — see §1, an adversarial re-check found ~20% on a
2-DOF arm for the 2012 IROS paper);
STEPPR (Sandia, IEEE TMech 2017) — the strongest *biped electrical* precedent —
selectively-engaging parallel joint springs cut **joint electrical power** (hip
ab/adduction −31% / 93 W; hip springs −13% overall robot power) **without RL**,
at the **hip and ankle, not the knee**. Note: these wins came from *clutched /
selectively-engaging* springs; the project's spring is **always-engaged**, so
those figures are an optimistic ceiling, not a transferable prediction.

**RL + elasticity (the learning frontier), all quadruped so far:**
- **Bjelonic 2023 (ETH RSL, RA-L)** — *closest on method.* Co-optimizes a
  parallel-elastic **knee** joint + locomotion controller via a
  design-conditioned RL policy + Bayesian Optimization; validates the **same
  torque-squared copper-loss proxy on real hardware** (33% torque-square
  efficiency, −30% peak torque, −31% joint electrical power, +11% runtime) on
  **ANYmal** (quadruped). [CoTr = ∫τ²dt/(mg·Δs).]
- **Bjelonic 2022 (DLR "bert")** — RL corrective actions on CPGs learn to exploit
  spring dynamics, but on a robot that **already has series springs** (controller
  only, no co-design).
- **PIL 2026 (Bristol, sim Unitree Go2)** — offloads up to 87% (flat) / 18%
  (rough) of **mechanical** power to parallel elastic joints, but **distills the
  spring from a trained policy** (not joint co-optimization) and reports
  **mechanical**, not electrical, power.

**Energy-efficient RL on humanoids — no physical spring, so not a scoop:**
- **Spoljaric 2025 (TU Wien)** — "variable stiffness" = **software PD-gain
  modulation**, no spring hardware; mechanical CoT.
- **ECO 2026 (BRUCE humanoid)** — constrained-RL energy efficiency on an
  **unmodified rigid** robot; absolute mechanical power, no compliance.

## 4 · Implications for this project

1. **Cite and differentiate the two danger papers up front** — Bjelonic 2023
   (quadruped, BO over a design-conditioned policy, not a single in-loop retrain)
   and the group's own Belov/Osokin 2024 (analytic optimization, fixed PD,
   leg-stand). Reviewers *will* find these.
2. **The hip-pitch pivot is well-supported by prior art.** STEPPR's biped wins
   were at the **hip and ankle**, not the knee — consistent with our own data
   (hip is an AC joint where a buildable linear spring captures ~55% of
   mean-square torque vs the knee's collapse to a constant). Moving Milestone 4 to the hip
   aligns the experiment with where parallel elasticity has actually paid on
   bipeds.
3. **The no-regen assumption is NOT supported by the literature** (that claim was
   refuted in the survey). It must be justified independently from real G1
   actuator/driver specs — not asserted. This raises the priority of the
   Kt/R/regen open item. *(Subsequently justified on engineering grounds — back-EMF
   below the ~48 V bus at locomotion speeds — in `running_program.md` / `CLAUDE.md`;
   that is the independent justification this point asked for, not a contradiction.)*
4. **The central empirical question is exactly the right one:** do Plooij/STEPPR
   wins (which used *clutched* springs) survive with an *always-engaged* spring
   once the gait can adapt in-loop? That post-hoc-vs-in-loop contrast is the
   methodological contribution; nobody surveyed has done it on a humanoid.
5. **Venue-tier lever:** upgrading from one hand-tuned spring to **co-optimizing
   spring parameters jointly with the policy** (differentiable-sim co-design, or
   BO à la Bjelonic) is the most likely path from RA-L to a top journal — and
   MJX being differentiable makes the gradient route genuinely on-trend.

## 4a · Spring-mechanism novelty — NOT novel (verified separately)

The proposed "two offset half-parabolic springs → tunable linear spring"
mechanism is an **exact known result**, scooped by **Hurst et al. 2004 (AMASC,
CMU)** — `F_eff = 4K·x₃·(x₂−x₁)`, with their pretension `x₃ ≡ (p₂−p₁)/2` — and
**Migliore et al. 2005**, and restated in **PMC10451064 (2024)**. Verdict:
**adopt and cite it, do not claim it.** Full derivation, citations, and the two
ways a mechanism-level contribution could still be *earned* empirically are in
[`mechanism.md`](mechanism.md). Novelty stays on the application stack (§1).
