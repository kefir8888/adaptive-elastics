## Our claim (what we are trying to position)

RL gait **co-adaptation** to a tunable **parallel** elastic element at the
**knee** of a **commercial rigid humanoid** (Unitree G1), evaluated in
**electrical** energy (copper loss ∝ τ², no-regen) rather than mechanical work,
with an explicit **post-hoc (fixed gait) vs in-loop (retrained gait)**
methodological contrast.

## Closest prior art (preliminary, unverified)

- **Skoltech ISR Lab — parallel-elastic knee paper.** Reportedly uses the same
  torque-squared electrical energy accounting, but **no reinforcement learning**
  and a narrow experimental scope. → If confirmed, closest on *energy method*,
  open on *learned co-adaptation*.
- **STEPPR (parallel-spring biped).** Parallel springs for efficiency, but
  reportedly **no gait co-adaptation / policy retraining** to the spring. →
  Open on *in-loop co-design*.
- **Duke Humanoid.** Passive-dynamics **RL for energy efficiency** — RL yes, but
  **no parallel-elastic knee element**. → Open on *the mechanism itself*.
- Clutched/switchable PEAs and parallel-elastic **exoskeletons** — typically
  **metabolic** (human) energy, not robot electrical; and clutched, not
  always-engaged.

## Gap (preliminary)

No located work combines all of: parallel knee elasticity + RL gait
co-adaptation + commercial humanoid + electrical-energy accounting + the
post-hoc/in-loop contrast. The constant-torque (preloaded) finding from our own
work loop (see `RESULTS.md`) may be an additional novel wrinkle vs the usual
linear-spring assumption.
